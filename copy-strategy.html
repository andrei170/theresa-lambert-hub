Copy Strategy · Theresa Lambert

Prepared 2026-05-11 by AG1 Consulting. The voice, framing, and headline patterns AG1 writes to for every Theresa Lambert deliverable.

Voice

The book voice is the through-line. Achieve with Grace: A guide to elegance and effectiveness in intense workplaces. Hospitality-elegance professionalism. Structured authority, calm under pressure.

Voice Pillars

  1. Structured. Sentences carry clauses. Comma rhythm. Reads like a book chapter, not a sales letter.
  2. Calm. No hype, no urgency theatrics, no all-caps. The reader feels lowered blood pressure, not raised.
  3. Operator. The author has run things. 8-figure boutique hotel. 90 to 180+ person team. Real constraints, real cadence.
  4. Hospitality-grade. Service language welcome. "Guest," "service," "cadence," "rhythm," "operation," "discipline," "grace."
  5. Invitational. CTAs are invitations to a conversation, not commands. "Explore," "discover," "apply," "book."

Voice Anchors (Use)

Words and phrases that belong:

  • elegance · effectiveness · effective · elegant · grace · graceful
  • intentional · structured · disciplined · calm · steady
  • service · cadence · rhythm · operation · operator
  • scale · growth · 10x · without overwhelm · sustainable
  • founder · operator · executive · entrepreneur · coach
  • mentorship · strategy · systems · consulting · Fractional COO
  • high-level · individualized · bespoke · curated · considered
  • Achieve with Grace · Diaries of a Six Figure Coach · Whistler

Voice Avoid (Cut)

Words and phrases that break register:

  • Banned AI-slop terms (all caught by the lint): next-level, paradigm shift, harness the power, elevate your, seamlessly, effortlessly, game-changer, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, transform your life, unlock your potential, revolutionary, delve into, navigate the complexities, in today's fast-paced world, at the end of the day, when it comes to, in the realm of, in conclusion, whether you're a seasoned, it's important to note, leverage synergies
  • Coaching-bro hype: "crushing it," "next level," "level up," "10x your life," "no excuses," "grind," "hustle"
  • Manufactured urgency: "limited time," "act now," "spots filling fast," "this won't last"
  • Triple-adjective stacks: "powerful, transformative, life-changing", pick one
  • "Not just X, but Y" pattern: banned
  • Em dashes, use comma or full stop or hyphen
  • All-caps shouting, never

Voice Cadence

  • Sentence length: vary, but lean longer than coaching average. 12-22 word sentences are the centre of gravity.
  • Paragraph length: 2-4 sentences. White space between paragraphs.
  • Punctuation: comma, colon, full stop. Avoid em dash entirely.
  • Bold: for emphasis on operator language, not hype. Use sparingly.
  • Italic: for the Achieve with Grace / Diaries of a Six Figure Coach titles and methodology terms.

Framing

The consolidated brand frame

ONE founder, two clearly-positioned tracks. The consolidation is the headline pivot. Every piece of public-facing copy after install reinforces:

"Theresa Lambert helps service-based founders + operators scale with elegance and effectiveness. Two tracks under one roof: Coaching (mentorship + signature programs) and Consulting (Fractional COO + Systems)."

The asymmetric credibility frame

Theresa scaled a boutique hotel to 8-figures at the #1 ski resort in North America before she ever called herself a coach. That story is the single most compelling proof in her portfolio. Every hero asset (homepage, hero VSL, LinkedIn bio, podcast intro, book inside-flap) opens or closes with it.

"Before I called myself a coach or a consultant, I scaled a boutique hotel to 8-figures at the #1 ski resort in North America."

The Achieve With Grace frame

The book is the methodology footprint. Every program, every consulting engagement, every LinkedIn essay rolls up to elegance + effectiveness in intense workplaces. The book is the through-line that links the coaching track and the consulting track under one operator identity.

The structured-Monday frame

Operator language. Hospitality-grade discipline. The Monday that runs without panic, the Friday that ends on time, the quarter that closes within plan. This frame replaces hustle-culture coaching with operator-cadence coaching.

Headline Patterns

Pattern 1 · The Asymmetric Proof Headline

"Before I called myself a coach, I scaled a boutique hotel to 8-figures."

Variations:

  • "Before I called myself a consultant, I led a team of 180 people through three transitions."
  • "Before I built coaching programs, I built the operating cadence for a $10M+ property."
  • "Before the podcast, the book, and the Mastermind, there was a hotel at the #1 ski resort in North America."

Pattern 2 · The Two-Track Headline

"Two tracks. One operator. Coaching for founders. Consulting for operators."

Variations:

  • "Mentorship for the founder building it. Fractional COO support for the founder scaling it."
  • "Strategy + mentorship if you're building the business. Systems + Fractional COO if the business is built."
  • "Two paths under one roof: where you are decides which one."

Pattern 3 · The Achieve-With-Grace Headline

"Scale without overwhelm. Grow without grinding. Achieve with grace."

Variations:

  • "Elegance and effectiveness in intense workplaces."
  • "Operator cadence for founders who'd rather not break the people who got them here."
  • "Hospitality-grade discipline applied to the business you've built."

Pattern 4 · The Diagnostic Headline (Quiz Entry Point)

"Where are you now? Three questions, one clear next step."

Variations:

  • "Founder, operator, or builder? The 3-question route map."
  • "Find the right next step for the stage you're in."
  • "The 90-second route from where you are to the right next conversation."

Pattern 5 · The Invitation CTA Headline

"A conversation, not a pitch. Book a time."

Variations:

  • "Walk through the plan together?"
  • "Apply to the next cohort."
  • "Explore Fractional COO."
  • "Start with The Way to $100K."

Cross-Asset Style Rules

  • First-person voice on founder-led assets (VSL script, podcast intros, About page, founder block on landing pages).
  • Third-person voice on third-party authority assets (Brainz Magazine bio block, podcast guest intros, speaker reels).
  • "You" in singular on all sales copy. One reader at a time.
  • Hospitality language preserved across every surface. The book voice is non-negotiable.
  • No emojis on the consolidated brand surfaces (homepage, landing pages, hub). Sparingly OK on social, never on conversion pages.
  • Numbers, not words, for revenue figures. "$172K/mo" not "one hundred seventy two thousand."

What This Strategy Optimises For

  • Recognition. A cold prospect who reads three Theresa pieces in a row remembers the same operator, not three different versions of her.
  • Trust. The hospitality-elegance register reads more like a partner than a marketer. Lower bounce, longer dwell.
  • Conversion. The 3-tier ladder + invitational CTAs route prospects to the right offer without manufactured urgency.
  • Compound. Every piece of copy reinforces the same brand frame. Twelve months of compounding consistency.
